## MIGRATION.md — the part that matters

An operator upgrading from v0.8.0 is told, in this order and in plain terms:

1. **Labels were being deleted after seven days, and the ones already gone cannot be recovered.**
   With the instruction to read the new `ORPHANED` figure in `dataset bias` to see the size of it,
   and the plain statement that nothing repairs it.
2. **Any retention policy set through the route was never persisted and must be set again.** With
   the asymmetry stated: the deletion it performed was real; the configuration was not.
3. **What the tiers mean now**, including the two behaviour changes an operator will actually hit —
   lowering `training_days` no longer destroys anything, and lowering `audit_days` is now the
   destructive control.
4. **What happens if the stored policy is unreadable**, and how to clear the warning.

## The security review

Continues from **F44**. Severity stated honestly: **high for data integrity, nil for
confidentiality, audit-chain integrity and access control** — the release's most consequential
defect *for the product's purpose*, and not a vulnerability. Inflating it would devalue the
F-numbers that are.

It enumerates **every path that can delete a human label after this release** — the audit sweep and
an explicit admin reduction, with what bounds each, who is attributed and what is recorded — and
states what cannot: `prune()`, the sink's dual bound, and the training tier at any time by anybody.

Four critical notes, including the one the brief called the useful one: **v0.8.0 audited the
lifecycle of the rows it created and not the lifecycle it inherited**, yielding a checklist item for
every future release that starts depending on another release's data.
