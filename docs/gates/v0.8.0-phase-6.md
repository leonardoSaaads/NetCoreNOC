| | capture OFF | capture ON |

| `dataset_pair` | 0 | **194 341** |

```
added rows per trap    : 62.24
added bytes per trap   : 6929
database growth factor : 9.88x
replay wall-time delta : +31.5%
```

`dataset_pair` reproduces Gate 0's 194 341 exactly, and `alarm`/`link` are unchanged — capture adds

**This is the worst case, not the typical one.** The corpus is **86 % storm** by construction with a
median of 100 candidates per activation; a quiet scenario like `background_noise` runs at a median
of 12. A steady-state regional ISP would see several times less. Recorded here, and carried into the
security review's critical analysis, so nobody quotes 62 rows/trap as *the* number.

## 4. Bounded by construction — and which bound actually binds

`test_the_sink_respects_both_bounds_and_the_dataset_does_not_grow` drives a synthetic storm and
asserts both bounds, and that **the dataset does not move while no label has arrived**.
`test_neither_sink_bound_can_reach_a_promoted_row` is the control that pays for DECISIONS #107's
one-table layout.

### The uncomfortable finding

At the measured 62.24 rows/trap, against the **2 000 000-row** default cap:

| traps/s | rows/day | cap binds after | 21 days would need |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 537 754 | **3.7 d** | 11 M |
| 1 | 5 377 536 | **8.9 h** | 113 M |
| 10 | 53 775 360 | **0.9 h** | 1 129 M |

> **The row cap is the binding bound in essentially every deployment. The 21-day age limit is a
> ceiling most installations never reach.** One 200 000-trap storm alone produces 12.4 M pair rows —
> **6.2× the cap**.

**Raising the cap is not the fix.** 21 days at 10 traps/s needs ~1.1 billion rows, roughly 124 GB.
That is not an appliance default. The cap is a **disk budget** (2 M rows ≈ 220 MB) and the honest
response is to stop implying otherwise:

* `dataset stats` now reports the **observed** `sink_window_days`, with the explanation that it is
  set by whichever bound binds first and that at most rates this is the row cap.
* The consequence — a label arriving after the sink has evicted its pairs records `coverage: none`
  — is exactly what §6.3's per-label coverage exists to make visible rather than silent.
* `CHANGELOG.md` and the security review carry it as a known limit.

This is the build prompt's *"if the measurement says the bound is not enough, the bound changes"*
clause resolved honestly: the bound's **value** is right for a disk budget; what changed is the
release's honesty about which bound governs.

## 5. Capture failure degrades capture, never ingestion

`test_a_capture_write_error_degrades_capture_and_never_ingestion` injects
`sqlite3.OperationalError` into `store.add_pairs` and asserts:

| | |
|---|---|
| traps ingested | **40 of 40** |
| `engine.db_errors` | **0** — a capture error is not a batch loss |
| `engine.capture.errors` | **> 0** — the failure is recorded |
| operator warning | *"…Ingestion was unaffected."* |

The alternative — letting the error propagate — would roll back the **whole batch**, so one bad row
could cost 500 traps. `test_a_prune_failure_degrades_capture_and_not_maintenance` covers the same
property on the maintenance path.

## 9. Retention

Ordering validated fail-closed with a precise reason (six parametrised cases, including the
`sink == training` equality); preview counts match what the delete then destroys on a crafted
fixture; the reduction is audited before/after; the maintenance loop touches only the sink; and the
policy in effect is recorded on `capture_run`.

**One real bug found here by the dead-code guard**, and it is the kind that would have been very
expensive in the field: `prune_dataset` had **no caller**. Applying a reduced retention stored the
policy and audited the change and *deleted nothing*. An operator would have set three months, seen
the count, confirmed, and kept twelve. Fixed, with
`test_the_retention_route_applies_and_audits_the_reduction` over HTTP.

### On the coverage figure

Gate 0 measured 95.89 % on the untouched tree. `receiver.py`'s coverage is **timing-dependent**
(87–91 % across runs of identical code), a limitation recorded on `docs/ROADMAP.md` since v0.7.3 —
confirmed again here by three consecutive runs of the *same* tree giving 18, 19, 18 missed lines.

An intermediate Phase 6 run measured **95.02 %**, genuinely below baseline: the new CLI subcommands
and the retention HTTP handler had no tests. Nine tests were added rather than the number argued
around, which is what took it to 96.02 %.

New-module coverage: `bias.py` 95 %, `bias_report.py` 98 %, `capture.py` 95 %, `labels.py` 93 %,
`store/dataset.py` 97 %.
