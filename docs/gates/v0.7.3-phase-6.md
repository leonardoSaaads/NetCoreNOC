## 1. The security review

**Zero findings**, and the review says so rather than manufacturing one to look diligent. Its length
goes on the three things that matter: the concurrency invariant that was the release's real risk,
the proof that nothing moved, and what the release does **not** buy.

Two sections are worth naming here because they are the ones a reader should not skip:

* **§1 — the invariant.** Why one `Store`/one connection/one lock is load-bearing rather than
  stylistic, the Phase 0 measurement that corrected the brief (the caches are *not* shared; the
  *connection* is, by 103 of 109 methods across 15 of 16 modules; and **no `Store` method acquires
  the lock at all**, which makes it a public contract of the object), and the mutation evidence that
  the guard actually fails on a real violation.
* **§6.1 — what the release makes worse.** A mixin split makes it *easier* for a future contributor
  to add a method that forgets the lock, because the neighbouring methods that would have shown the
  pattern now live in another file. The controls are the concurrency test and `write_txn`, **not**
  the layout. Stated plainly, with the specific gap named: neither control catches a *store method
  that a caller forgets to wrap*, and that gap existed at v0.7.2 too.

`threat-model.md` is **unchanged**. No trust boundary moved, no actor was added, no asset changed,
no assumption was altered. Saying so is the right answer.

Both **specified and deferred**, not fixed — `api/declare.py` is a file this release does not touch,
