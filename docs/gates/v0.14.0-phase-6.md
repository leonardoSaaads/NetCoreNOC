# v0.14.0 — Gate 6

**The simulated network: the registered shape, built, and measured for content.**

`PREREGISTRATION-0.14.0.md` §5.1 fixes what the generator produces, in what proportion, from which
seed, and §5.4 forbids changing any of it after a verdict is seen. No verdict has been seen at this
gate. What follows is the shape as registered, and the measurement that says whether it has any
content — because six adversarial names prove nothing and Part IX's Gate 6 says so in one sentence:

> report, per shape, how many pairs fall within ±0.15 of the threshold, because a "pegadinha" that
> produces no near-threshold pair is a scenario with a name and no content.

## 2. The proportions are exact in every increment, not on average

Twenty incidents and shares of 30/20/15/15/10/10 % divide with no remainder, so the quota is
**6, 4, 3, 3, 2, 2** and it holds in *each* increment rather than in expectation.

A sampled proportion would be right on average and wrong in every increment. Since §5.4 forbids
changing the shape after a verdict is observed, "right on average" would be a shape nobody could
reproduce — and reproducing it is the whole of what makes the demonstration honest.

`test_the_proportions_are_the_registered_ones` transcribes the plan's table **by hand** rather than
importing the generator's quota: a test that read the number it is checking would agree with itself
whatever that number became.

## 3. The one design decision this phase had to make, and it was forced by the clock

The plan does not say how incidents are separated. The first design spaced them 900 s apart so their
correlation windows could not overlap, and **it cannot be driven over real UDP**: `receiver` stamps
a trap on **arrival**, so replaying an eight-hour fixture in eight minutes collapses every incident
into one 120 s window and the whole network becomes a single situation. The clock belongs to the
appliance and the generator does not get to inject it.

So the incidents are **concurrent and separated by network element** — which is also what a real NOC
looks like at 3 a.m. It works because of what the correlator actually computes: on an appliance that
has learned nothing, a cross-NE pair scores `0.30·decay + 0.35·0 + 0.35·0 ≤ 0.30`, under the 0.50
threshold, while a same-NE pair takes the structural `entity_affinity = 1.0` and scores at least
`0.35 + 0.30·decay`. Same NE links, different NEs do not — **until** the learner has seen enough
co-occurrence to move `A` and `E`, which is precisely the drift the near-threshold shapes exist to
produce.

**This is a choice about the driving mechanism, not about the registered shape.** The six shapes,
their proportions, the seed and the labelling rule are the plan's and are unchanged.

## 4. The merge chain: what the measurement caught, and what it changed

§5.1 registers *"a situation merge chain of length ≥ 2"*. The first implementation produced chains of
length **1**, and the measurement is the only reason anybody knows that.

Two causes, both structural and neither guessable from reading:

1. **`engine._assign_situation` keeps `min(sids)`** — the lowest situation id always survives. So
   bridging the lowest pair first gives two edges that both point at the survivor: two merges, chain
   length one. A chain of length 2 needs the **lowest id absorbed last**:

   ```
   t=0    role 0 alarms      -> S1   (lowest id)
   t=6    role 1 alarms      -> S2
   t=12   role 2 alarms      -> S3
   t=20   bridge S2 and S3   -> S3.merged_into = S2     (S2 < S3)
   t=26   bridge S1 and S2   -> S2.merged_into = S1     (S1 < S2)
   ```

2. **`learn.entity_affinity` returns zero for a cross-NE pair until `E` has mass ≥ `MIN_EDGE_N` (5)**,
   and `entity` is `UNIQUE (ne_id, parent_id, key)` so two devices can never share an entity id.
   Giving each merge-chain incident **fresh** NEs meant every ring pair was seen once and `E` stayed
   at zero forever — so nothing could ever bridge two NEs and the chain was unbuildable. The ring's
   three NEs are therefore **fixed and reused across increments**, two rings so the two slots per
   increment do not merge into each other.

Both are recorded here rather than quietly fixed, because the second one is the interesting half:
**the chain appears only once the appliance has learned enough**, which is the honest behaviour of a
system that starts knowing nothing, and it is reported rather than engineered away.

`DATA-LINEAGE.md` §4 records that **no corpus in this repository holds a merge edge at all**, which
is why v0.10.1's byte-frozen reports could not have verified incident-identity consolidation.
`store/shadow.py` says it from the other side: `COALESCE(s.merged_into, f.situation_id)` is **one
hop**, and only a chain of length ≥ 2 tells a one-hop resolution from a transitive one. This closes a
gap open since v0.10.1.

### 5.1 The number worth pausing on

`PREREGISTRATION-0.10.0.md` §0 records that **the champion accepts 99.83 % of the pairs it
evaluates** on the frozen eval corpus, and that this is why the discriminating population was
missing. On this network it accepts **43 748 of 117 895 — about 37 %.**

That is the corpus difference stated as one number. The eval corpus is ten scenarios of which only
two contain both classes; this network is built so that most pairs are a real decision. It is also
the honest limit of the comparison: **a corpus this release generated is not evidence about a
network, and §6 of the plan says so three ways.**

## 6. Determinism

Byte-identical across two processes, asserted by `test_the_generator_is_byte_identical_across_two_processes`
— across processes rather than twice in one, for `test_challenger.py`'s reason: hash randomisation,
dict ordering and import order are per-process and a within-process repeat sees none of them.

No RNG anywhere. Every draw is a SplitMix64 finaliser over `(seed, incident, salt)` — **a pure
function, not a stream** — so nothing depends on how many draws came before it. `forest.py` makes the
same choice for the same reason, and `shadow_cv._Lcg`'s docstring records what the alternative cost
this project once: a two-cluster bootstrap that alternated 0, 1, 0, 1 and reported a zero-width
interval.

## 7. The separation, asserted rather than promised

§1 extends the `incumbent_linked` prohibition to the simulator:

> The scenario DSL knows the correct `situation_key` of every event. A label the machine produced
> does not judge the machine, whether the machine is the champion or the generator.

Two tests, and the second is the belt to the first's braces:

* `test_no_runtime_module_can_reach_the_simulator` parses **every** module under
  `src/netcorenoc/` and fails on any import of `simulation` or `scenario_dsl`. Parsed, not read —
  because reading is what let `model_version.py` reach the challenger for three releases without
  anyone noticing (F57).
* `test_no_promotion_path_module_mentions_a_ground_truth_field` asserts that `promotion.py`,
  `judge.py`, `shadow_cv.py` and `evaluation_folds.py` do not so much as **name** `situation_key`,
  `entity_key` or `is_root`, so a copy-paste would be a failing diff.

The bare word *truth* is deliberately not among the three: `judge.py` writes "truthiness" in a
paragraph about `Verdict` being an enum, and a substring check that fired on that would be a guard
nobody could keep green — F51's `_SKIP_DIRS`, one release later.

### What this gate does not establish

* **Nothing about whether the corpus will clear the floors.** That is Phase 7's measurement and
  §5.3's stopping rule — including the branch where ten increments do not clear them, which §8.3
  registers **in advance** as a successful outcome.
* **Nothing about model quality, on any network.** §6 of the plan: every verdict from this corpus is
  a demonstration of the machinery and never a claim.
