# v0.10.0 — Gate 1: reproduction and scope

**Reproduce before you build.** The three facts of the brief's Part I are not background; they are
why this release has the shape it has, and a build that cannot reproduce them is a build working
from someone else's numbers. Each is reproduced **by execution** here, and each carries a control
that had to behave the other way.

Everything below runs on the tree at
`c03aef0181554c0c71482e57d03677f25964c3a5ac20a7bf1b1d74bff1ba1e01`'s commit — the ratified plan, and
no v0.10.0 code.

**The headline of this gate is a disagreement**, and it is in §2: the plan's minimum-detectable-
difference table is reproduced at large `n` and **not** at small `n`, by two independent methods that
agree with each other. Per the brief, it is reported rather than resolved.

---

## 1. Fact 1a — the cluster bootstrap interval width

**Reproduced.** A cluster bootstrap over incidents, 2 000 resamples, rate 0.70, percentile method;
the median 95 % interval width over 400 simulated corpora. Pure stdlib — no numpy, no scipy.

**The model, stated because the plan does not state it and a reader must be able to check it:** each
incident contributes **one** bag-level outcome, and the bootstrap resamples **incidents** with
replacement. That is what makes it a *cluster* bootstrap — the unit resampled is the unit the
correlation lives in, not the pair.

| incidents | plan | observed | delta |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 0.500 | **0.500** | +0.000 |
| **37** | **0.289** | **0.297** | +0.008 |
| 50 | 0.246 | **0.240** | −0.006 |
| 100 | 0.180 | **0.180** | +0.000 |
| 500 | 0.079 | **0.080** | +0.001 |

Every figure lands within Monte-Carlo noise of the plan's. **The model is confirmed**, which matters
more than the individual numbers: it tells the estimator of Workstream 3 exactly what to implement.

**Controls.**

```
CONTROL — the width must FALL as n rises, or the estimator is measuring nothing
  strictly decreasing: True
CONTROL — a degenerate corpus (rate 1.0) must give width 0, not a plausible number
  width at rate 1.0: 0.000
```

The second is the one that matters. A bootstrap that returned a plausible-looking interval on a
corpus with no variance at all would be manufacturing uncertainty, and every width above would be an
artefact of the code rather than a property of `n`.

> **At 37 incidents the 95 % interval on a single rate is 0.289 wide — ±14.5 p.p.** No pair of
> scorers over the same three features is separated by that.

## 2. Fact 1b — the minimum detectable difference. **The plan's table does not reproduce at small `n`.**

### 2.1 The closed form

Normal approximation on the difference of two proportions measured over `n` clusters each:

```
delta = (z_{alpha/2} + z_power) * sqrt(2 * p(1-p) / n)
      = (1.959964 + 0.841621) * sqrt(2 * 0.7 * 0.3 / n)
```

Documented, dependency-free, and evaluated at the same `p = 0.70` the plan's §3.1 bootstrap uses.

### 2.2 The disagreement

| incidents | plan | closed form | direct Monte-Carlo | closed form − plan |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 0.42 | **0.524** | **0.52** | **+0.104** |
| **37** | **0.25** | **0.298** | **0.33** | **+0.048** |
| 120 | 0.16 | **0.166** | 0.18 | +0.006 |
| 300 | 0.10 | **0.105** | 0.11 | +0.005 |

**The large-`n` half reproduces and the small-`n` half does not.** At 120 and 300 incidents the
closed form agrees with the plan to within a rounding step. At 37 it is 5 p.p. higher — a **19 %
relative** discrepancy — and at 12 it is 10 p.p. higher, **25 % relative**.

**Two independent methods agree with each other and disagree with the plan.** The "direct
Monte-Carlo" column is not the closed form re-expressed: it searches upward for the smallest true
difference at which a two-sided z-test on two simulated clustered proportions rejects at least 80 %
of 600 trials. It shares no arithmetic with the closed form, and it lands at or above it everywhere
— the small excess at low `n` is the discreteness of a 12- or 37-observation binomial making the
test conservative, which is the direction one would predict.

### 2.3 What is reported, and what is not changed

**Neither side is adjusted.** The brief is explicit: *report the disagreement rather than adjusting
either side; one of them is wrong, and finding out which is worth more than a table that matches.*
The plan is ratified and hash-guarded, so it could not be edited even if that were desirable; and
tuning the closed form until it reproduced 25 p.p. would be fitting a formula to a table, which is
the same error in a smaller register.

**One diagnostic, offered as a hypothesis and not as a conclusion.** The `p` at which the closed
form returns exactly 0.25 at `n = 37` is **p = 0.821** (or 0.179):

```
n=37, p=0.50 -> 0.326
n=37, p=0.70 -> 0.298
n=37, p=0.95 -> 0.142
the p that would make n=37 give exactly 0.25:  p = 0.821
```

Solving the same way at `n = 12` for 0.42 gives **p ≈ 0.832**. Those two agree closely with each
other and **not** with the `p` implied by the large-`n` rows (≈ 0.72 at 120, ≈ 0.73 at 300). So the
plan's four figures are not the output of one closed form at one `p`; the two halves of the table
behave as though they were produced under different assumptions. That is a description of the
symptom, not a diagnosis, and it is as far as this gate goes.

### 2.4 Why the direction matters, and why this does not weaken the plan

**The disagreement runs in the direction that makes the plan's own argument stronger.** The plan
concludes:

> *At 37 incidents the judge cannot decide anything about model quality. No plausible pair of
> scorers over the same three features differs by a quarter.*

If the true detection threshold at 37 incidents is **30 p.p. rather than 25**, that sentence is
*more* true, not less. Every registered consequence — the power condition as a verdict trigger, the
`INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` expectation of §7.1, the decision not to spend the seal — is unaffected or
reinforced. **Nothing in the plan depends on 25 being the right number**; it depends on the number
being far larger than any difference this project could produce, and both candidate values are.

Carried into the v0.10.0 security review as an opinion for v0.11.0, which is where §9 of the plan
directs a disagreement.

### 2.5 Controls

```
CONTROL — the MDD must FALL as n rises
  strictly decreasing: True
CONTROL — the closed form must move with p; a formula ignoring it would be a constant
  n=37, p=0.50 -> 0.326
  n=37, p=0.70 -> 0.298
  n=37, p=0.95 -> 0.142
```

The second control is the important one, and it is the Appendix B rule applied: a "closed form" that
returned the same answer whatever `p` was would reproduce a monotone table by accident and could
never have exposed §2.3's finding.

## 3. Fact 2 — adaptive selection. **Reproduced exactly, on all six figures.**

**The model:** `q` candidates, **all with the same true rate**, each measured on the same `n`
incidents; the reported figure is the best one seen. The entire gap between the reported rate and
the truth is therefore selection noise **by construction** — there is no real difference to find.
4 000 runs per cell.

| incidents | queries | plan median | observed | plan p90 | observed p90 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 4 | +0.133 | **+0.133** | +0.217 | **+0.217** |
| **37** | **12** | **+0.111** | **+0.111** | **+0.165** | **+0.165** |
| 120 | 12 | +0.067 | **+0.067** | +0.100 | **+0.100** |

**Six numbers, six exact matches to three decimal places.** The mechanism is not merely
plausible-looking; it is the plan's mechanism.

**Controls.**

```
CONTROL — ONE query cannot inflate a rate; the median must be ~0, not merely small
  n=37, queries=1 -> median +0.0027   p90 +0.0838
CONTROL — inflation must RISE with queries and FALL with n
  n=37,  q=1  -> 0.003
  n=37,  q=12 -> 0.111
  n=37,  q=48 -> 0.165
  n=370, q=12 -> 0.038
```

The first control is the one that makes the rest meaningful. A single query is a plain unbiased
measurement, so its median inflation must be **zero**, not "small" — if a one-query run had shown
+0.05, every figure above would have been a bug in the harness rather than a property of selection.

> **Four releases (v0.10.0 → v0.13.0) tuning against one holdout is the middle row.** An
> eleven-point improvement produced entirely by *looking* is larger than any improvement this
> project is likely to produce by *learning*. **This is why the seal is built and not spent.**

### 4.3 The controls — and the one that matters most

**A zero is the most dangerous measurement this gate makes.** *"Measuring nothing and concluding
CLOSED"* is on this repository's record (Appendix B), so the zero above is paired with a probe that
must come back **non-zero** using the **same census code**.

The control corpus is the identical mechanical policy with one addition: a `split` on a bag of four
or more members also marks its first two.

| | real corpus | control corpus |
|---|---:|---:|
| labelled bags | 41 | 41 |
| `split` bags | 13 | 13 |
| **`asserted_negative_pairs`** | **0** | **1 474** |
| **`asserting_bags` (registered)** | **0** | **2** |
| coverage distribution | full 25, partial 6, none 6, empty 4 | identical |

**The census code can see an assertion. The zero is therefore a property of the corpus, not of the
query.**

And the control carries its own finding, which is the sharper version of v0.9.1 §7.5: even when
*every* eligible `split` is marked, only **two** bags assert anything, because only two of the
thirteen have four or more members. `1474 = 2·(240−2) + 2·(501−2)`. **Two asserting bags against a
floor of fifty**, from a corpus where the labelling rule marked everything it could.

Second control:

```
CONTROL — coverage must not be a constant; a single-valued column would make the
          exclusion rule vacuous and the count meaningless
  distinct coverage values observed: 4 ['empty', 'full', 'none', 'partial']
```

**The ten excluded bags** (`coverage IN ('none','empty')`, plan §2.6(c)): **6 `none` + 4 `empty`
= 10 of 41**, reproduced exactly. A `none`-coverage bag yields an all-singleton partition in which
every asserted negative pair is satisfied for free, so excluding them is what stops a perfect number
being produced by nothing happening.

> **Fact 3 confirmed. The judge cannot be exercised on the corpus and must be demonstrated on a
> purpose-built fixture — exactly as v0.9.1's exclusion set had to be.**

## 5. Workstream 1 — the census the brief asks for, including what it reveals about the guard

### 5.1 Incident identity: one hop versus transitive, on this corpus

```
merge edges : 4   {29: 28, 30: 28, 31: 28, 33: 32}
chain depths: [1, 1, 1, 1]
max depth   : 1

incidents, one hop (COALESCE) : 37
incidents, transitive         : 37
cycles detected               : 0
pre-v0.8.0 merges (status='merged' with no destination) : 0
situation status: closed 35, merged 4, open 2
```

**41 bags → 37 incidents, a reduction of 4**, and the reduction from the one-hop count is **zero**.

> **Every merge chain in this corpus is exactly one hop long, so `COALESCE` and a transitive
> resolution return the same 37 incidents.** The corpus cannot demonstrate the guard Workstream 1
> exists to build.

This is the same shape of finding v0.9.1 recorded about the exclusion set, and it has the same
consequence: **the transitive resolution and its cycle guard must be demonstrated on a purpose-built
fixture**, and Phase 2's gate must say so rather than quoting "37 = 37" as though it were evidence.

**Pre-v0.8.0 merges are counted, not assumed absent** (plan §3.3): **0** on this corpus, measured as
situations in `status='merged'` carrying no `merged_into`. §7.10's trigger — pre-v0.8.0 merges
exceeding 10 % of `asserting_incidents` — is therefore not fired here, but the quantity exists and
is reported rather than assumed.

### 5.2 The control that separates the two resolutions

### 5.3 The three scope populations, never averaged

| population | definition | observed |
|---|---|---:|

Eight labels were written under a **restricted** scope, and every one of them hid nothing, so all

**empty on this corpus**, which means that rule too can only be demonstrated on a fixture.
