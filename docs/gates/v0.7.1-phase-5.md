## 6. With a policy active: no read, no write, no influence, no escalation

An end-to-end scenario against a live appliance — the fiber-cut estate on `127.0.0.0/24` plus a
wholly separate incident on `192.168.50.1`, with viewer and editor scoped to `127.0.0.0/24`:

```
in-scope situation=1   out-of-scope situation=3   out-of-scope device id=3
scope policy applied: 200

--- cannot READ outside scope ---
  /api/entities      clean
  /api/graph         clean
  /api/timeline      clean
  /api/situations    clean
  /api/stats         clean
  GET /api/situations/3  -> 404 (indistinguishable from absent)

--- cannot WRITE outside scope ---
  feedback   -> 404 {'detail': 'no such situation'}
  close      -> 404 {'detail': 'no such open situation'}
  label      -> 404 {'detail': 'no such label target'}
  nonexistent -> 404 {'detail': 'no such situation'}  (identical body)

--- cannot INFLUENCE learned state outside scope ---
  50 posts: pair mass 1.000000 -> 1.000000, epoch 0 -> 0

--- cannot ESCALATE by writing a label ---
  /api/me scope {'scoped': True, 'ne_count': 2} -> {'scoped': True, 'ne_count': 2}

--- in-scope work still works ---
  feedback   -> 200
  close      -> 200

--- admin is never scoped and can always repair ---
  admin scope: {'scoped': False, 'ne_count': None}
  admin reads the out-of-scope situation: 200;  policy cleared: 200

audit chain: ok=True checked=62
audited denials: [('feedback', 'denied', 52), ('label.set', 'denied', 2), ('situation.close', 'denied', 1)]
```

Three things in that transcript are worth naming:

- **The out-of-scope feedback body is byte-identical to the nonexistent one.** That is the F34
  property, and it holds because the scope denial routes into the handler's pre-existing 404 branch
  rather than adding a second one.
- **Every denial is audited** — 55 rows across the three actions — so a scoped principal probing the
  write surface leaves a trail even though the response tells them nothing.
- **A partially-visible situation stays writable.** An earlier draft of this scenario scoped to a
  single address and was surprised by a 200 on the fiber-cut situation. That was correct: the
  situation has a member on the in-scope NE, so "at least one member in scope" makes it visible
  *and* writable — the same predicate on both sides, which is the whole point of reusing
  `project_situation_detail`'s rule rather than restating it.
