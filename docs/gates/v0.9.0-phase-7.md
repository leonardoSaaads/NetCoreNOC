## 2. The security review's four honest notes

Three were required; there are four, and they are the part of the document worth reading.

1. **What the agreement number licenses.** It is agreement, not correctness; on a *uniform* bag it
   licenses nothing at all, because such a bag contained no decision; and **the number this release
   published for its own corpus is not a measurement of operators** — it came from a mechanical
   labelling rule and measures the instrument.
2. **Was the discriminating population large enough? No.** 13 `split` bags of 50 required, **5**
   mixed of 20, and **exactly one bag both `split` and mixed**. Every quality figure in the shadow
   report is from a synthetic fixture, and the report and the gates say so.
3. **A deviation from the plan, reported rather than edited.** `same_oid_root` was registered and is
   unbuildable — it cannot be served, and an unserveable feature guarantees skew. Four free
   parameters instead of five, and **the floor stayed at the registered fifty** rather than
   rescaling to forty, because softening is the direction DECISIONS #114 forbids.
4. **Which threshold I would argue is wrong, as an opinion for v0.10.0.** The `split`-bag floor is
   the wrong quantity; the population that carries information is `split ∧ mixed`, which the plan
   does not floor at all. Recorded in the review, **not** by editing the plan — which is exactly
   what §9 of the plan directs and what `tests/test_preregistration.py` makes visible.

## Closing note

The release did what the brief asked for and the answer was *not yet*. Both headline numbers are
negative results: the corpus cannot support a challenger, and the population where the champion
could have been wrong is about an eighth of what gets labelled. The machinery is built, gated,
deterministic and isolated — and the honest thing this release leaves behind is a measurement that
makes the case for shrinking the four releases after it.
