# v0.14.0 — Gate 7

**The chain, end to end. The floors were not met, and this is what happened instead.**

`PREREGISTRATION-0.14.0.md` §5.3 registered two stopping conditions before any corpus existed. The
second one is what this release reached:

> * every floor of `PREREGISTRATION-0.10.0.md` §2.2 is met and the judge has returned a verdict; or
> * **ten increments have been generated without every floor being met**, in which case the release
>   reports the shortfall and the demonstration is recorded as incomplete.
>
> **The second branch is a successful gate outcome and the report says so.** A loop that cannot stop
> without success is a loop that will manufacture one.

§8.3 says the same thing from the other side, and it was written before any of this was measured:

> **8.3 — Ten increments without every floor met (§5.3).** The demonstration is incomplete, the
> shortfall is reported per floor, and the release ships the three kinds without the end-to-end
> proof. **This is a gate outcome, not a failure**, and the report leads with it rather than burying
> it.

So this gate leads with it.

| | after ten increments | floor | short by |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `asserting_bags` | **10** | 50 | **40** |
| `asserting_incidents` | **10** | 30 | **20** |

**The champion did not change, and no promotion was applied.** The seal was never spent — on the
demonstration database or anywhere else. What the release ships is the three kinds, fitted on this
corpus with recorded fingerprints, and a diagnosis of exactly why the corpus could not clear the
floors. The diagnosis is the most valuable thing the phase produced and it is §3.

## 2. Two paths, and the numbers are compared rather than assumed equal

§5.3 step 1 is *"boot a real appliance on an empty database, **real UDP**"* and step 3 is *"label
through `POST /api/situations/{sid}/feedback`, the route the console calls, **never by writing to the
store**"*. Both are taken literally by `eval/simulation/drive_http.py`:

| | `drive.py` (the census) | `drive_http.py` (the witness) |
|---|---|---|
| the appliance | `Engine` in this process | **`python -m netcorenoc.main` in a subprocess** |
| traps | `parse_trap` on real wire bytes | **a UDP socket, one per source address, at 1×** |
| labels | `engine.apply_feedback` | **`POST /api/situations/{sid}/feedback`, bearer token** |
| principals | three `principal_ref`s | **three service tokens, `token:<id>`, RBAC + audit row** |
| the artefact | fitted in process | **`netcorenoc promotion register` — the CLI** |
| the gate | — | **`POST /api/promotion`, admin, server-derived** |
| increments | **ten** | three |

Ten increments over a real socket is about half an hour of wall clock and cannot be compressed: the
receiver stamps a trap on **arrival**, so `spread_beyond_tau`'s 95-second gap is 95 seconds of real
time, and a time scale that shortened it would be generating a different shape. So the ten-increment
census is the in-process path's and the real-surface path is the witness that the two agree on the
increments they share.

### 2.1 The one thing this host cannot do, stated rather than hidden

The generated devices are in `10.0.0.0/8` and **a UDP source address has to be bindable**. This host
has no interface in that block:

```
10.11.1.1: [Errno 99] Cannot assign requested address
127.1.1.1: BOUND ok -> ('127.1.1.1', 44967)
```

`tools/trap_replay.py`'s `Sender` swallows that with `contextlib.suppress(OSError)`, which is right
for a burst generator and catastrophic here: every trap would arrive from `127.0.0.1`, the whole
network would collapse onto one NE *before the correlator saw it*, and nothing would fail. So the
harness rewrites the address **for transport only** — `10.a.b.c` is sent from `127.a.b.c` — and
`appliance.Sender.socket_for` binds without suppressing, so an unbindable source is an exception
rather than a silently different network.

`test_the_transport_rewrite_is_a_bijection_on_this_corpus` asserts injectivity over every device an
increment actually produces, rather than arguing it from the second octet's range. The generated
document is byte-identical either way; only the datagram's source field differs.

**This is on the release's list of what was not verified on real hardware.**

They differ by **one confirmed bag in increment 0**, and the honest reading is that the difference is

asserting count, because a pure bag is never asserting. It is recorded rather than reconciled: the
two paths are not the same experiment and the gate does not claim they are.

### 2.3 What the witness establishes that the census cannot

* **The receiver accepted every datagram**: 855 received, **0 dropped, 0 quarantined, 0 denied.** The
  wire encoding, the v2c parse, the community tagging and the allowlist all held for 855 real packets
  from 25 distinct source addresses.
* **The labels went through the route**, with a bearer token per principal, RBAC on every call, scope
  resolution, `Exclusion` reconciliation and an audit row — none of which the in-process path touches.
  The `excluded_reconciled` the floors are counted from is the server's own number, arrived at from
  ids a console would have rendered.
* **The CSRF check fired and was satisfied honestly.** `POST /api/tokens` on a cookie session returned
  403 until the harness sent the `Origin` and `X-NetCoreNOC-Client` headers the shipped console sends.
  The bearer clients do not send them, because a token is not sent by a browser — the harness carries
  each principal's real headers rather than the union.
* **The rate limiter fired and was waited out, not disabled.** Every principal here comes from
  `127.0.0.1` and so shares one bucket of 30 burst / 10 per second. A driver that turned the limiter
  off would be demonstrating a different appliance, so `appliance.Http` backs off and retries — which
  is what a console does — and a 429 that will not clear still raises.
* **The artefacts were registered through the CLI**, because there is no HTTP route that creates a
  model version. That is a design decision rather than a gap: *the thing that could put a new model in
  front of traffic is not reachable from the network.*
* **The gate refused all three, server-derived, and wrote its reasons.** Four triggers each,
  `seal_query_count` 0, and a `promotion` row per refusal.

### 2.4 The defect this walk found, and it is the reason the walk was worth doing

The three verdicts above were, in the first run of this loop, **derived from an arm that had nothing
to do with the model being proposed.** `_derived_inputs` measured `engine.shadow.scorer` while
`propose_promotion` activates the `model_version_id` the request named, and nothing bound the two
together — no check that the candidate's parameters were the shadow scorer's, and `params_hash`
reaches `promotion.evaluate` only as a fold-materialisation key.

That is **F59**, it is fixed in this release, and ADR #195 records the decision. Three releases of
tests had not caught it because **no test had ever proposed a candidate that differed from the shadow
scorer** — every fixture registered the logistic model that was already in shadow. It is not visible
from reading `routes_promotion.py`, because each half is locally correct; it is visible from
registering a `tree` and watching a logistic verdict come back.

The numbers in §2.2 are from the re-run **after** the repair. They are identical to the pre-repair
run, and they would be: the floors are unmet, so the verdict is `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` whatever the
metrics say. That is also why fixing it here is not adaptive selection — the repair cannot change any
verdict this release reports.

### 3.1 The arithmetic that makes the floor unreachable

Under the registered labelling rule an **asserting** bag is a `split` carrying
`excluded_reconciled >= 1`, and a `split` happens only when a bag holds two or more ground-truth
situation keys. So:

> **mixed bags is an upper bound on asserting bags.** 10 mixed bags, 10 asserting bags — the bound
> is tight, and 50 was never reachable from 134 bags of which 124 are pure.

The interesting number is not 10. It is **230 members and 24 truth keys in one bag**: an entire
increment's twenty concurrent incidents, six different registered shapes, merged into a single
situation. Twelve bags per increment that should have been separable were, instead, one.

### 3.2 The mechanism, step by step

Six links in the chain, each one measurable and each one measured:

1. **The window is in "storm" nearly all the time.** `correlate.Window.process` sets
   `storm = len(self.index) >= STORM_ALARMS` — window *occupancy*, not a situation's size. Twenty
   concurrent incidents put ~285 alarms into a 120-second window, so the flag is on far more often
   than it is off.

2. **A storm deposits mass on every NE pair in the window.** `Learner.observe_pairs` walks
   `{other[1] for other in others if other[1] != new[1]}` — *every distinct other NE currently in the
   window* — and adds `STORM_DAMPING` (0.1) to each pair.

3. **56 ONUs on one OLT clear the only guard.** `storm_concealing` emits `PON_LOS` plus 56
   `PON_DYING_GASP` on a single OLT inside about three seconds. All 57 carry that OLT's NE, so each
   deposits 0.1 on `(OLT, X)` for every other NE `X` in the window: **57 × 0.1 = 5.7**, and
   `learn.MIN_EDGE_N` is **5.0**.

4. **Past the guard, the NPMI is ~1 and only the evidence discount limits it.** During a storm every
   alarm co-occurs with every other, so `p_ab ≈ p_a ≈ p_b`, the normalised PMI saturates at 1.0, and
   `Matrix.npmi`'s `discount = m/(m+1)` is the only thing left. The measured series is exactly that:
   0.8333, 0.8571, 0.8750, 0.8889 — that is 5/6, 6/7, 7/8, 8/9 — and the largest observed is
   **0.9714 = 34/35**, beside a largest recorded pair mass of **34.000**. The two numbers agree to
   the last digit, which is how a hypothesis stops being one.

5. **The score follows.** `s = 0.30·decay + 0.35·A + 0.35·E`. With two incidents concurrent, `decay`
   is near 1; with `E` at 0.83 the cross-NE score is **≈ 0.85**, rising past 0.93 as the mass grows.
   The design intends a cross-NE pair on a cold appliance to score `0.30·decay + 0 + 0` — at most
   **0.30**, under the 0.50 threshold. Recorded verbatim from the `link` rows of increment 0:

   ```
   i0(10.11.1.1) -- i12(10.14.1.13)  score=0.5333 T=0.3000 A=0.2333 E=0.0000 dt=0.0s
   i0(10.11.1.1) -- i12(10.14.1.13)  score=0.8507 T=0.2965 A=0.2625 E=0.2917 dt=0.3s
   i0(10.11.1.1) -- i12(10.14.1.13)  score=0.9339 T=0.2975 A=0.3182 E=0.3182 dt=0.2s
   ```

   `10.11.1.1` is an `independent_overlap` incident; `10.14.1.13` is a *different* incident's storm
   OLT. They share nothing. **2 546 of the 3 063 cross-incident links are that one shape pair.**

6. **`class_affinity` has no `MIN_EDGE_N` gate at all.** `Learner.entity_affinity` refuses to return
   a learned value below mass 5.0; `Learner.class_affinity` is `return self.A.npmi(a, b)`, unguarded.
   The first row above is that asymmetry on its own: `E = 0.0000` — the entity guard holding — and
   `A = 0.2333` (raw 0.667 = 2/3, so mass 2) already pushing the pair over 0.50 without it. The
   measured maximum class term is 0.3412, raw **0.975**.

### 3.3 `STORM_DAMPING` does not prevent this, and the reason is arithmetic

`learn.py` documents `STORM_DAMPING` as *"10x smaller updates during mass storms"*, and it is the
protection a reader would expect to cover exactly this case. It does not, for two reasons:

* **It delays the crossing; it does not prevent it.** A storm large enough to matter is large enough
  to clear a threshold of 5.0 at one-tenth weight. 56 ONUs is not an extreme storm — `learn.py`'s own
  `STORM_ALARMS` is 50, so 56 is barely one.
* **It damps the numerator and not the denominator.** `observe_pairs` takes the weight;
  `observe_activation` calls `observe_occurrence(item)` with the default 1.0, so the marginals and
  the activation total are undamped. NPMI is a ratio, and damping only one side of it does not scale
  the result the way the docstring's "10x smaller updates" implies.

**The guard that is working is `MIN_EDGE_N`.** 1 276 of 1 357 NE pairs never reach it — the median
pair mass is 0.300, two orders of magnitude below the threshold. It holds for every ordinary pair
and is defeated only by a storm, which is precisely the traffic it most needs to hold for.

### 3.4 This is issued as a finding, not fixed here

It goes to `SECURITY-REVIEW-0.14.0.md` as **F58**, unfixed. Three reasons, and the third is the one
that decides it:

* **It is not v0.14.0's scope.** The release's prime directive is that `correlate.py`, `engine.py`,
  `receiver.py`, `capture.py` and `learn.py` end byte-identical to v0.13.0, verified by hash. A fix
  lives in `learn.py`.
* **It is not obviously a defect.** An appliance that has watched two NEs alarm together 34 times
  *should* raise its opinion of that pair. Whether a storm's co-occurrence deserves the same weight
  as an ordinary one is a modelling question with a real answer on either side, and it is not one to
  settle inside a release that was measuring something else.
* **Fixing it here would be adaptive selection with the correlator as the knob.** §5.4's reasoning
  applies with full force: changing the data-generating process after seeing a verdict is worse than
  tuning a model, because a model's tuning is recorded in `params_document` and this would be
  recorded nowhere. Changing the *correlator* after seeing the verdict it produced is the same move
  one layer down.

### 3.5 What was NOT changed in response to the shortfall, and this is the point

§5.4 is explicit and it was obeyed to the letter:

> The generator's shape (§5.1), its proportions, its seed, the labelling rule (§5.2) and the
> increment size are fixed here and are not changed after any verdict is observed. The loop may
> generate **more** data of the registered shape. It may not change the shape, the proportions, the
> seed, or the labelling rule.

| | changed after the verdict was seen? |
|---|---|
| the six shapes and their internals | **no** |
| the proportions 30/20/15/15/10/10 | **no** |
| the seed `20140000` | **no** |
| the labelling rule | **no** |
| the increment size (20 incidents) | **no** |
| the ten-increment ceiling | **no** |
| the floors (50 / 30) | **no** — imported from the server |
| the correlator, the learner, the thresholds | **no** — byte-identical, §6 |

The obvious rescue was available and was not taken: separating the incidents in time instead of by
network element would have stopped the storm co-occurring with the other nineteen incidents and would
almost certainly have cleared both floors. `docs/gates/v0.14.0-phase-6.md` §3 recorded the
concurrent-by-NE decision **before** any verdict was seen, with its reasons; changing it afterwards
would be exactly what §5.4 exists to forbid, whether or not the word "shape" covers it. It goes to
the security review as an opinion **for v0.15.0**, which is where §5.4 sends it:

> If the build concludes that the registered shape is wrong, that conclusion goes into the security
> review as an opinion **for v0.15.0**, and the demonstration is reported against the registered
> shape whatever it produced.

### 3.6 A correction that corrected nothing, recorded because it did not

Mid-phase the simulated operator's truth lookup was re-keyed from the generator's `entity_key` to
`(device, trap OID)`, on the reasoning that `alarm.instance` holds the engine's own heuristic at
level 0 and an entity-key lookup would therefore miss. The reasoning is right and the key is the
right one.

**It changed nothing.** The loop was re-run and produced byte-identical output — the same 10
asserting bags, the same shortfall. The shortfall it was meant to explain was never caused by it, and
the real cause is §3.2. It is recorded here, and in `labelling.truth_of`'s docstring, because a fix
that fixes nothing is worth exactly one sentence saying so — and because the alternative is a reader
finding the reasoning later and believing the problem was solved.

## 4. Steps 7 and 8 did not happen, and could not

§5.3 steps 7 and 8 are *"an admin approves a promotion"* and *"the champion changes; verify by
observation that subsequent situations carry the new model's provenance."* Neither is reachable from
`INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`: `promotion.evaluate` refuses to apply, `store.set_active_model_version` is
never called, and the pointer does not move.

**That refusal is the machinery working.** The gate was proposed, it derived every input server-side,
it refused, and it wrote the refusal with its reasons to the `promotion` table — which is the audit
question that table exists to answer. What the release cannot claim is the *other* half: that a
champion change propagates correctly to a subsequent situation's provenance. §8.7 registers the
outcome where it does not, and this release has no observation either way.

**`docs/releases/` will say so in one line and not bury it.**
