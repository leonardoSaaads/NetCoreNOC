## 3. The four decisions this gate had to make, and what they resolved to

Each is an ADR; each followed the decision protocol rather than a preference.

| Question | Protocol rule | Resolved to |
|---|---|---|
| is the asserted-negative count *about the client* or *about the evidence*? | ambiguity resolves to **about the evidence** | tier 2 — reconciled (#131) |
| store the reconciled count, or derive it later? | ambiguity resolves to **store**, if any input is subject to retention | store both eagerly (#132) |
| may the scope-of-marking column be backfilled? | ambiguity resolves to **no**, unless every input is stored evidence no retention path removes | no, `NULL` forever (#133) |
| what does the drift check do on a disagreement? | — | report, never correct (#134) |
| how is a truncated assertion counted? | mixed populations are reported separately, never averaged | its own population (#135) |

**#132's answer is not a preference; it is forced by a measurement.** Gate 0 §4 established, on one
clock, that the reconciled count's inputs survive `prune()` and tier 3's input does not. Under a
lazy/eager split, two quantities of the same assertion would have different availability profiles
forever.

## 4. One thing this gate changed relative to the brief, and why

The brief specifies the property test as `0 ≤ m_reconciled ≤ n` plus component non-negativity plus
the sum. Gate 0 §2a measured something the brief's framing does not supply:

> at `n = 60, m = 30`, **every component is non-negative and the identity closes**, while the label
> asserts nothing at all.

So component non-negativity is **necessary and not sufficient**. It discriminates `m > n`; it does
not discriminate *"`m ≤ n`, and every one of those marks names an alarm outside the bag"*. What
discriminates the second case is the intersection itself.

Nothing in the plan changes — the three assertions are still the three assertions — but the
**framing** does, and it is recorded in ADR #136 and will be recorded in the test's own docstring: the
property is asserted over the *reconciled* count, where the bound holds by construction, so the test
is a guard against that construction being undone rather than a filter applied to a client number.
A reader who takes non-negativity as the whole repair has missed the half that matters.

## 5. The remainder side: decided here rather than discovered later

The brief asks that the release *"consider — and decide, and record the decision"* whether the
remainder deserves the same treatment as the marked side.

**Decided: record the marked side only, and state explicitly what that does not cover.**
`EVIDENCE-BOUNDARY-0.9.2.md` §10 carries the decision, the arithmetic, and the gap. The short form:
`scope_redacted_members` already bounds the remainder side, so a reader holding `n`, the reconciled
count `m`, the redacted-member count `h` and the new column `b` can bound the observable pairs
without a further column — and on Gate 0 §3.C's measured case the bound is **exact** at `[2, 2]`,
which is the number that case actually has. A second stored column would be a convenience with a
maintenance cost, and ambiguity about scope resolves to a ROADMAP line.
