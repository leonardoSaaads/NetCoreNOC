# The evidence boundary

*Written in v0.9.2, before the code that implements it. It is the document v0.10.0 should cite when
it decides whether the asserted-negative count may carry a pre-registered floor.*

---

## 0. The sentence

> **A quantity that describes the client may be derived from the client. A quantity that describes
> the evidence must be derived from the server's own reconciliation. The reported set never reaches
> an arithmetic operator whose result is reported as a property of the network.**

That is the whole document. Everything below is either the vocabulary for saying it precisely, the
enumeration of which of this repository's inputs fall on which side, or the argument for the three
design choices that follow from it.

The rule exists because it was broken. `docs/gates/v0.9.2-phase-0.md` §1 records an ordinary
`editor`, over HTTP, in one request, moving the corpus-wide asserted-negative total by **+900**
while asserting **nothing** — thirty marked ids, none of them naming a member of the bag, and
`learn.penalize` moving not one matrix cell. The quantity v0.10.0 is most likely to promote into a
sufficiency floor was being computed from something the subject controls, and a floor computed from
something the subject controls is not a floor.

---

## 1. Three tiers

| Tier | What it is | Trust | Who may read it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Reported** | what the client said: verbatim, ordered, bounded, never rejected | **untrusted** | reports **about the client** only |
| **2 — Reconciled** | tier 1 ∩ the server's own bag, computed by the server at the moment of the verdict | **authoritative** | every quantity about the evidence |
| **3 — Interpretable** | whether the reconciled marks were about members the labeller could observe | **authoritative, and its input expires** | every quantity that will decide anything |

### 1.1 Why `client_diverged` is correct and `asserted_negative_pairs` was not

Both are computed from client input. Only one of them is a defect, and the difference is the whole
rule.

`client_diverged` compares the client's reported bag against the server's and records that they
differ. It is a **measurement of the client**, and a client that lies about its own bag has told you
exactly the thing that metric exists to record. There is no reconciliation to perform, because the
subject of the sentence *is* the untrusted party.

`asserted_negative_pairs` claims to measure the **network**: how many pairs of alarms an operator
has asserted do not belong together. Its subject is the evidence, so its inputs must be evidence.

If you cannot say which of those two sentences a quantity is, you do not yet know what to do with
it, and no amount of clamping will help.

### 1.2 The vocabulary, borrowed and not imported

The naming discipline is worth taking from **W3C PROV**'s entity / activity / agent split — as
*vocabulary only*, with no dependency, no serialisation format, and no library. Each stored quantity
should complete this sentence:

> *The **entity** X was **attributed to** the **agent** A, and was **generated by** the **activity**
> Y performed by the server.*

* The report is an **entity** attributed to an **agent** who is the client.
* The reconciliation is an **activity** performed by the server, at a named instant.
* The reconciled count is an **entity generated by** that activity and **derived from** both the
  report and the server's bag.

**If a column cannot be described in that sentence form, its provenance is not yet clear enough to
store.** That test is the reason `excluded_reconciled_source` exists: without it, a row cannot say
whether its reconciled count was generated by the write-path activity or by the migration's, and
those are two different acts by two different actors at two different times.

## 2. Every client-controlled write-path input, classified

Re-derived from the tree in `docs/gates/v0.9.2-phase-0.md` §5, not copied from any brief.

### 2.1 The label write path

`POST /api/situations/{sid}/feedback` and `POST /api/situations/{sid}/close` share
`routes_operate.label_context`, so the classification is one table for both.

| Input | Becomes | Tier of the derived quantity | Verdict |

| `excluded_ids` | `feedback_exclusion` rows, verbatim and ordered | **1** — the report itself | **legitimate**: this is the evidence, stored as evidence |
| `excluded_ids` (its length) | `feedback.excluded_count` → three multipliers | claimed to be **2**, actually **1** | **F46** |
| `excluded_ids` (its length) | `feedback.excluded_truncated` | **1** — a fact about the report | **legitimate**: the bound must be visible in the data |
| `excluded_ids` ∩ the server's bag | `Exclusion.marked_positions` → `learn.penalize` | **2** | **correct since v0.9.1** — the one consumer that was right |
| `remainder_together` | `feedback.remainder_together` → `remainder_asserted` | **1** about the operator's own claim; its **denominator** is the partial-split population | **repaired by F46's fix** |
| — | *whether the marks were about members the labeller could see* | **3** | **F47** — the quantity does not exist |

### 2.2 Everything else on a write path

`LabelIn`, `RetentionIn`, `LoginIn`, `PasswordIn`, `UserIn`, `RoleIn`, `TokenIn`, `ConfigIn`,
`ScorerParamsIn`, `ScorerRollbackIn`, `PolicyIn`. **None of these becomes a quantity that describes
the evidence.** They are configuration, identity, or the governance documents themselves. Each is
bounded by its Pydantic field constraints, and the two that name a network object are already
guarded by a finding's regression test — F35 for `LabelIn.label` (the operator label left the scope
resolver's input set entirely) and F37 for `LabelIn.id` (a label may only name a target that
exists, and a missing one answers the same 404 an out-of-scope one does).

**`ScorerParamsIn` is the closest call and it is worth stating why it is not tier 1.** Its five
floats decide what the correlator links, so a number the client supplies genuinely influences the
system's behaviour. But it does not *describe* anything: it is a **configuration of the appliance**,
recorded as a versioned, audited, rollback-able row (`0005_scorer_config.sql`), reachable only by
`admin`, and never reported as a measurement of the network. The evidence boundary governs
*measurements*; the scorer panel is governed by the change-control machinery built for it. Recorded
here because a future reader will ask, and because §5 of this document names it as the input this
release is least confident about.

### 2.3 The structural asymmetry that produced both findings

`feedback_member` carries a `source` column precisely so that trusted and untrusted rows stay
distinguishable at every layer downstream. **`feedback_exclusion` carries no such column**, because
it did not need one — every row in it is the client's, by construction. What was missed is that the
*denormalized counter on the label row* then inherited the client's trust **without ever saying so**.

Migration `0010` argued the separate-table decision well, and argued the denormalization well. What
it did not do is carry the **trust marker** across the boundary it created. A column whose trust is
implicit in a table's name is a column whose trust is lost the moment a value is copied out of it.

**The reports must print both numbers.** A line saying *"asserted negative pairs"* and a line saying
*"client reported marks"* are different lines, and after this release the difference between them is
a **diagnostic**: it is how an operator learns whether their corpus was ever written by something
other than the shipped UI. That disagreement count is the first number the report prints.

## 4. The three design choices, and why each is the only defensible one

### 4.1 Reconciliation happens at write time, and is stored

Tier 2 alone could be a join, computed lazily forever — both of its inputs survive every retention
path. Tier 3 cannot: `docs/gates/v0.9.2-phase-0.md` §4 measured `alarm.ne_id` being collected one
retention pass after a situation closes, while the label, its exclusion rows and its server bag all
survive.

Two options follow, and only one is defensible.

* **Compute tier 2 lazily and tier 3 eagerly.** Two quantities belonging to the same assertion then
  have different availability profiles, and every consumer for the rest of the project's life has to
  know which is which.
* **Compute both eagerly and store both.** This also keeps migration `0010`'s own denormalization
  rationale intact, in its own words: *"a report that had to join to count would not stay cheap, and
  a corpus whose exclusions were pruned would lose the count as well as the members."*

The second. **The denormalized number stays; what changes is which side of the trust boundary
produces it.**

### 4.2 The reconciliation is verifiable against its source, and drift is reported, never corrected

Modern practice on denormalized aggregates is uniform on one point: the normalized tables remain the
source of truth, the denormalized value is a **rebuildable copy**, and the system carries a
reconciliation query plus drift monitoring rather than trusting the copy. Applied literally: the
maintenance pass recomputes tier 2 from `feedback_exclusion ∩ feedback_member(source='server')` and
compares.

**It reports. It does not correct.** A silent correction destroys the evidence that a write path is
broken, which is the entire reason this release exists. If the drift check had existed in v0.9.1 and
had been a *corrector*, F46 would have been invisible: every hostile row would have been quietly
repaired on the next maintenance pass, the reports would have looked right, and the write path
would have stayed broken forever. Drift is surfaced as an operator warning and an audit row, and
the operator decides.

### 4.3 Constraints are the last line, not the only line

The invariant `0 ≤ reconciled ≤ member_count` is enforced at every layer where it is tractable:

1. **the request model** — already bounded, and deliberately *not* validating (§6);
2. **the store method** — which computes the intersection rather than accepting a count;
3. **the schema** — `docs/gates/v0.9.2-phase-0.md` §7 established by experiment that SQLite 3.45.1
   accepts an enforced `CHECK` on `ALTER TABLE ... ADD COLUMN`, on `INSERT` and `UPDATE` alike;
4. **the maintenance drift check** — because the same experiment established that a `CHECK` is
   **not retroactive**, so the constraint bounds the future and the drift check bounds the past.

A constraint violation should be **unreachable** and should still be **enforced**. If one ever
reaches the database, that is then unambiguous evidence of a bug in a layer above, rather than an
ambiguity about which layer was supposed to be responsible.

## 5. What is deliberately *not* changed, and why the temptation is wrong

**The client's report is still recorded verbatim, still bounded, still never rejected, and still
never used to validate the existence of anything.** F34, F35, F37 and F39 are not being reversed.
`feedback_exclusion`, `feedback_member(source='client')`, `excluded_count` and `excluded_truncated`
keep their exact present semantics and their exact present bytes.

**Reconciliation is an additional quantity. It is never a replacement and never a filter on what is
stored.**

The tempting alternative — reject ids that are not in the bag, or silently drop them before storing
— is strictly worse, and it is worth being precise about why. A response that differs when a
supplied id names a real alarm is an **existence oracle**: a scoped editor who cannot see alarm 4711
learns whether it exists by watching the status code, the body, or the timing. That is F34's finding
class, closed once and proved closed by `tests/test_partial_split.py` §4 in status, body **and**
timing. Trading an evidence-quality problem for an existence oracle is a bad trade, and it would be
a bad trade even if the evidence-quality problem were larger than it is.

A build that starts rejecting ids has misread this document.

### 5.1 What F47's repair does *not* fix

It does not stop a scoped editor from making a blind assertion. **It makes the assertion legible.**
Preventing it would mean rejecting client ids, for the reasons directly above. After this release, a
consumer can *see* that an assertion was made partly about members its author could not observe, and
can weight, stratify or exclude accordingly — which is precisely what the scope fingerprint was
added for in v0.8.0, one level down.

### 5.2 Truncation, and what an incomplete assertion may not be

`excluded_truncated = 1` means the client's list was cut at `MAX_CLIENT_MEMBERS`. After
reconciliation, a truncated report may **under-count** the real marks: the ids beyond the bound are
gone, and some of them may have named members of the bag.

**A truncated assertion is therefore incomplete, and an incomplete assertion may not silently serve
as a denominator.** Truncated rows are reported as **their own population**, counted and named,
which is what this project already does with every other mixed population — `capture_provenance`,
`acquisition_channel`, and the three populations of §6 below. They are not excluded (that would
discard evidence) and they are not averaged in (that would let a known-incomplete count set a
threshold).

## 6. The three populations this buys, and the rule about averaging them

After this release a corpus divides into three populations a reader can name:

* **clean** — `scope_redacted_members = 0`, so no mark could have been about a hidden member;
* **checked** — a restricted scope, and the new column says **how much** of the assertion was blind;
* **unknown** — written before migration `0011`, and permanently so.

**They are reported separately and never averaged**, exactly as `acquisition_channel` is
(DECISIONS #126). An aggregate over the three would destroy the characterisation retroactively for
rows already written, which is the failure `0010`'s channel column was created to prevent.

**Zero is a real answer and must be distinguishable from "not recorded".** A restricted-scope label
where every reconciled mark was about a visible member records `0`, and that is a strong statement.
A row written before `0011` records `NULL`, and `NULL` means unknown **forever** — never `0`. This
is derivation versus fabrication (§7) at the level of a single cell.

## 7. Derivation, fabrication, and the line between them

> **Recomputing a quantity from evidence already stored is derivation, and may be backfilled.
> Inventing a quantity whose input is gone is fabrication, and must be `NULL` — counted and reported
> as unknown, never assumed zero.**

Applied to this release:

| Quantity | Inputs | Do any retention paths remove an input? | Backfillable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| the reconciled count | `feedback_exclusion` ∩ `feedback_member(source='server')` | **no** — both survive `prune()`, measured | **yes**, and marked as backfilled |
| the scope of the marking | `alarm.ne_id` for each marked id, plus the policy in effect *at label time* | **yes** — `alarm` rows are collectable one pass after close | **no**, and there is no exception |

The second row has a second reason beyond the clock, and it is the stronger one: even on a database
young enough that every `alarm.ne_id` still exists, the *scope policy in effect at label time* is
not recoverable. `governance_policy` is append-only and `feedback.scope_policy_id` records which
document was active, so a determined reconstruction could get close — but "close" is a
reconstruction of what the policy **said**, not of what the operator **saw**, and the gap between
them is exactly the thing tier 3 exists to record. A backfill here would be manufacturing an
observation.

The same discipline `HONEST-JUDGE-0.10-DRAFT.md` applies to the three irrecoverable pre-v0.8.0
merges, applied to a new column.

## 8. The two findings, with their severity stated honestly

### F46 — the asserted-negative count is the client's list length

**Severity: high for evidence integrity. Nil for confidentiality. Nil for availability. Explicitly
not a privilege escalation.**

The reasoning, stated so a reader does not have to take the rating on trust:

* **Not a confidentiality issue.** Nothing is disclosed. The response is byte-identical and
  timing-identical whether a marked id names a real alarm, an alarm the principal cannot see, or
  nothing at all — proved in v0.9.1 by `tests/test_partial_split.py` §4, and re-measured in
  `docs/gates/v0.9.2-phase-0.md` §1.
* **Not an availability issue.** The write is bounded at `MAX_CLIENT_MEMBERS = 512` per label, the
  bound is recorded, and no path here raises.
* **Not a privilege escalation.** The `editor` capability is required and is not exceeded. An
  `editor` may already label any situation with at least one visible member; this changes what the
  *corpus* records about that label, not what the principal may do.
* **High for evidence integrity**, because the corrupted quantity is the one most likely to become a
  **decision input** — a pre-registered sufficiency floor in v0.10.0 — and because the dangerous
  failure is silent. A negative total is loud. `+900` from zero real assertions is not.

### F47 — the assertion does not record whether it could have been made

**Severity: high for evidence integrity. Nil for confidentiality. Nil for availability. Explicitly
not a privilege escalation.** Same reasoning, plus one more:

* **Scoping is a presentation control by design**, and this project has said so since v0.7.1
  (DECISIONS #58/#59, and `test_governance.py::test_f32_scoping_is_not_tenant_isolation_is_
  documented`). A scoped editor labelling a situation they can only partly see is *the specified
  behaviour*; v0.8.0 added the scope fingerprint precisely so that it would be interpretable. F47 is
  that fingerprint being **incomplete** — it records how much of the *bag* was hidden and not how
  much of the *assertion* was blind.

**Neither finding can be produced by a label written by the shipped UI**, which sends only ids it
rendered. The corpus's exposure is therefore an empirical question, answered in
`docs/gates/v0.9.2-phase-0.md` §6: zero rows, on every corpus this repository can construct. **That
is a fact about sequencing, not a property of the system** — the write path accepted a hostile label
from an ordinary `editor`, over HTTP, in one request.

## 9. What this licenses, and what it still does not

**Licensed after this release.** A consumer may treat the reconciled count as a statement about the
network: it is `|reported ∩ the server's own bag|`, computed by the server at the instant of the
verdict, from a bag the client cannot influence, and bounded by `0 ≤ m ≤ n` at four layers.

**Not licensed, and worth being explicit because the gap is where the next finding lives.**

1. **The reconciled count does not mean the operator was *right*.** It means they marked members
   that existed in the bag. Whether those members truly belonged to another situation is the thing
   the corpus is evidence *for*, not something the corpus can validate.
2. **A reconciled count of `m` on a restricted-scope label does not mean `m·(n−m)` observable
   pairs.** `docs/gates/v0.9.2-phase-0.md` §3 measured 9 reported, 8 reconciled, and **2** between
   two members the operator could actually see. Tier 3 records the marked side; the *remainder* may
   also contain redacted members, and §10 below says exactly what is and is not covered.
3. **The pre-`0011` population is permanently uninterpretable for tier 3**, and it must be counted
   rather than assumed away.
4. **Nothing here makes the corpus large enough to decide anything.** Sufficiency is v0.10.0's
   question and this document does not answer it.

## 10. The remainder side: decided, and what the decision does not cover

A partial split asserts `marked × rest`. Tier 3 as specified records **how many of the reconciled
marks were about members outside the labeller's visible set** — the *marked* side only.

**The decision: record the marked side, and say plainly what that does not cover.**

What it does not cover: the `rest` may also contain redacted members, and a pair is unobservable if
**either** end is. `docs/gates/v0.9.2-phase-0.md` §3.C measured the gap — 8 reconciled pairs of
which only 2 join two members the operator could see — and every one of the other 6 is unobservable
because of the *remainder* side, not the marked side.

Why the marked side alone is nonetheless the right thing to store:

* **`scope_redacted_members` already bounds the remainder side.** A reader holding
  `member_count = n`, `excluded_reconciled = m`, `scope_redacted_members = h`, and the new column
  `excluded_reconciled_out_of_scope = b` can bound the observable pairs without a further column:
  the marked-and-visible count is `m − b`, the remainder holds `n − m` members of which at most
  `h − b` are hidden, so observable pairs lie in
  `[(m − b) · (n − m − (h − b)), (m − b) · (n − m)]`. On the measured case that is `[2, 2]` —
  exact — and it is exact whenever the bounds are tight.
* **A second stored column would be a second thing to keep correct**, and this release's own thesis
  is that stored quantities need a named, auditable derivation. One column with a stated derivation
  is better than two, one of which is a convenience.
* **Ambiguity about scope resolves to a ROADMAP line**, and an exact stored count of
  *unobservable asserted pairs* is a candidate for one if a consumer ever needs it without the
  arithmetic above.

## 11. What this document does not decide

* **The floor unit for v0.10.0.** Whether a sufficiency floor is expressed in asserted negative
  pairs, in labels carrying an assertion, or in something else — and over which of §6's three
  populations — is v0.10.0's to choose **deliberately and in advance**. Choosing it here would be
  the pre-registration error wearing a different hat. `HONEST-JUDGE-0.10-DRAFT.md` carries an
  inheritance note stating what became available; it chooses nothing.
* **Whether `client_diverged`'s ordered comparison should become order-insensitive.** It is a
  legitimate measurement of the client either way; its imprecision as a measurement of *staleness*
  is a ROADMAP line.
* **Whether client ids should ever be rejected.** §5 says no, and says what would have to become
  true to revisit it. `docs/security/SECURITY-REVIEW-0.9.2.md` §4 answers the question directly.
